          How it works

          1. Mirror. Every buyer, device, IP and merchant becomes a node. Orders from PayPal checkout and webhooks add edges, so the twin is always a copy of the live business.
          2. Attend. A 2-layer GAT (4 attention heads, then 1) learns how much each neighbour matters. A new account sharing a device with a known ring lights up even if it looks clean on its own.
          3. Decide. GAT risk is combined with transaction signals (new device, geo mismatch, spend spike, velocity) into approve, review or block before the PayPal order is created.
          4. Attack. The red team forks the twin and adapts attacks until something gets through.
          5. Rehearse. The what-if simulator replays traffic and attacks under a proposed policy, so you change the real one with evidence.

          Written from scratch in NumPy with hand-derived backprop; tests check every gradient against finite differences.
